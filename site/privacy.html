Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 August 2026

LubeLogger mobile is an unofficial, open-source Android companion app for a self-hosted LubeLogger server that manages vehicle maintenance records. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to LubeLogger or Hargata Softworks.

This policy explains what data the app handles. In short: the app has no analytics, no advertising and no account you create with the developer, and everything it does while you use it stays between your device and the LubeLogger server address that you configure.

There is one exception, and only you can trigger it: if you decide to send a report from inside the app, what you write is published as a public issue on GitHub. Nothing is sent unless you choose that, and the whole path is described under Sending a report.

Who the app talks to

  • Your LubeLogger server — the self-hosted address you enter during setup. While you use the app, this is the only server it talks to. There is no cloud backend, no analytics service and no advertising SDK.
  • The report relay and GitHub — contacted only when you open the report form and choose to publish. The relay (app-relay.morganmlg.com) is a small Cloudflare Worker operated by the developer; it exists so that you can file a report without having a GitHub account. If you never send a report, the app never contacts either of them.

Data stored on your device

  • Server address and API key for your LubeLogger server. The API key is stored in the Android Keystore-backed encrypted storage on your device and is never sent anywhere except to your own server, over the connection you configure.
  • App settings and preferences — such as display units, which record tabs are shown, and notification preferences — stored in local app storage.
  • A random installation identifier — a UUID the app makes up for itself the first time you open the report form. It is not a device identifier and is not derived from one; its only job is to pace reports, so that whoever keeps sending them waits longer each time. Reinstalling the app replaces it.
  • A diagnostic log, while a recording is running or waiting for you to decide what to do with it.

This data is removed when you uninstall the app or clear its storage.

Permissions and why they are used

  • Internet — to reach the LubeLogger server you configure, and the report relay if you choose to send a report.
  • Notifications — used only if you opt in to reminder notifications, so the app can alert you when a maintenance reminder is due or overdue.
  • Background scheduling / run at startup — when reminder notifications are enabled, the app periodically checks your server for due reminders in the background and re-registers that check after a device restart. No data leaves your device beyond the connection to your own server.

Files and attachments

When you attach a document or photo to a record, the file you pick is uploaded to your LubeLogger server and attached to that record. When you open an attachment, it is downloaded from your server to your device so it can be viewed. Files are exchanged only with your own server.

Diagnostic logs

The app can record a diagnostic log, but only when you start one yourself from the report form. Nothing is recorded before you press start, and a recording stops by itself after 15 minutes, or sooner if it reaches 10 MB.

Such a log describes what the app did: screens you opened, controls you pressed, requests to your server and the status codes that came back, what the background reminder check decided, and errors. Before anything is written down it goes through a redaction pass, which removes:

  • your API key and anything else shaped like a token, password, authorization header or cookie;
  • user names and e-mail addresses — a LubeLogger instance is often shared by a household or a small fleet, and naming the other accounts in a public issue has never been part of a diagnosis;
  • the host part of your server address. What is kept is its shape — whether it is http or https, whether the host is a name or an IP address, and the port — because that half is often the diagnosis, while the address itself is yours.

A control you press is recorded under an internal name rather than the label shown on screen, and text you type into the app is not recorded.

You review the whole log on screen, and then you choose one of two destinations:

  • Save to a file — the log is written where you pick and stays on your phone. Nothing is uploaded, and whether you ever send that file to anyone is entirely your decision.
  • Report on GitHub — the log is published, as described in the next section.

Once the log has been saved, published or discarded, the app deletes its own copy.

Sending a report

This happens only when you write a description in the report form and send it. It is voluntary from beginning to end: if you do not send a report, nothing described in this section takes place.

Three kinds, and only one carries a log

  • A bug report is about something the app did wrong, so it carries the diagnostic log you have just reviewed.
  • A change or feature request is an argument about what the app should do, which no recording settles. These carry no log at all, and the information about your setup is cut down to the app version, the LubeLogger version your server reports, and your device's language.

What leaves your phone

  • Your description, exactly as you wrote it, in whatever language you used.
  • The diagnostic log you have just reviewed, compressed — for a bug report only.
  • A short session header: the app version, your Android version, your device's language tag (for example pl-PL), the LubeLogger version your server reports, and the shape of your server address as described above.
  • The installation identifier described above.
  • Your IP address, which the relay necessarily sees, as does any server you connect to.

Where it goes

The relay checks the report, commits the log file — when there is one — to the bug-report-assets branch of the app's public GitHub repository, and opens a public issue that quotes your description and the session header and links to the log. The app then shows you the address of that issue.

A public issue is public and permanent by its nature. Anyone can read it, search engines index it, and other people can copy or quote it. Deleting an issue later cannot undo a copy somebody has already taken. Please treat the description as something you are publishing: do not put your name, e-mail address, home address, licence plate, network details or anything else you would not post publicly into it.

What the relay stores

The relay's job is to keep this endpoint from being abused, and it holds as little as it can to do that:

  • Your installation identifier, your IP address and your description are turned into keyed hashes (HMAC-SHA-256) before anything is written down. The relay never stores or logs any of the three in the clear.
  • Those hashes, with timestamps, sit in a small counter database used for the hourly and daily caps, the per-address cap and duplicate detection. Report rows are deleted automatically within 25 hours, and the rows that pace repeat reporting within 48 hours.
  • The relay keeps no copy of your log or your description. Both are forwarded to GitHub, and from then on they exist there and nowhere else.
  • When a cap is hit, the developer receives an alert over Telegram. It carries counters only — never any part of any report.

Services involved

  • Cloudflare — hosts the relay and filters abusive traffic at the edge. It processes connection metadata, including your IP address, in the course of delivering and protecting the request.
  • GitHub — hosts the public issue and the log file attached to it.
  • Telegram — carries the maintainer alerts described above, which contain no report content.

Children

LubeLogger mobile is a utility app for vehicle owners and is not directed at children.

Data deletion

No data is collected on any server operated by the developer, apart from the short-lived counters described above, which expire on their own and cannot be traced back to you. To remove all locally stored data, uninstall the app or clear its storage in Android settings. Data held by your LubeLogger server is governed by that server, which you control. If you have published a report and want the issue taken down, write to the address below — bearing in mind that removal cannot reach copies already made.
